### What Digital Markets Act proceedings involving Apple are disclosed?

Apple Inc. is disclosed to be involved in two Digital Markets Act (DMA) investigations by the European Commission (EC):

- On March 25, 2024, the EC opened a formal noncompliance investigation against Apple under Article 5(4) of the DMA. This investigation relates to how developers may communicate and promote offers to end users for apps distributed through the App Store, as well as how developers may conclude contracts with those end users. [1]

- On June 24, 2024, the EC opened an additional formal investigation against Apple regarding whether the Company’s new contractual requirements for third-party app developers and app marketplaces may violate the DMA. This is referred to as the Article 6(4) Investigation. [1]

On April 23, 2025, the EC fined Apple €500 million in the Article 5(4) Investigation and issued a cease and desist order requiring Apple to remove technical and commercial restrictions that prevent developers from steering users to alternative distribution channels outside the App Store. Apple has appealed the EC’s Article 5(4) decision. The EC also issued preliminary findings in the Article 6(4) Investigation. If the EC makes a final determination that there has been a violation in the Article 6(4) Investigation, it can issue a cease and desist order and may impose fines up to 10% of Apple’s annual worldwide net sales. The EC may also seek to impose additional fines if it deems that Apple has violated a cease and desist order. [1]

Apple believes that it complies with the DMA and has continued to make changes to its compliance plan in response to feedback and engagement with the Commission. [1]
